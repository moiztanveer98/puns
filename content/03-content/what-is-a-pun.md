# What Is a Pun? Definition, Types & Examples

A pun is wordplay that uses a word's double meaning, or a word that sounds like another word, for humor. People use puns to add humor to jokes, headlines, conversation, and writing, because the twist rewards a listener the instant they catch it.

## What Is the Definition of a Pun?

A pun is a form of wordplay that exploits either a word's multiple meanings or its similarity in sound to a different word, creating a humorous or clever double meaning. The twist is the point: a pun only works if the reader notices both meanings at once, not just one.

## How Does a Pun Work?

A pun works through one of two mechanisms: polysemy, where a single word genuinely has two meanings ("I'm reading a book on anti-gravity — it's impossible to put down"), or homophony, where two different words sound alike ("Time flies like an arrow; fruit flies like a banana"). Both mechanisms rely on the listener holding two meanings in mind at the same moment the sentence resolves.

## What Are the Types of Puns?

Puns fall into several named categories based on exactly how the double meaning is built. [Homophonic puns](/homophonic-puns/) use words that sound alike but mean different things, [homographic puns](/homographic-puns/) use words spelled the same with different meanings, and several other types — homonymic, compound, recursive, visual, and texting puns — each build the twist differently. See [Types of Puns](/types-of-puns/) for the full breakdown of all seven, with examples of each.

## What Are Examples of Puns?

These five examples show the double-meaning mechanism at work in different everyday lines.

- "I used to be a baker, but I couldn't make enough dough" plays on the double meaning of *dough* (bread ingredient and money).
- "A bicycle can't stand on its own because it's two-tired" plays on the sound-alike between *two-tired* and *too tired*.
- "I'm reading a book on anti-gravity — it's impossible to put down" plays on the double meaning of *put down* (set aside and stop, physically).
- "The past, present, and future walked into a bar — it was tense" plays on the double meaning of *tense* (grammatical term and emotionally strained).
- "I used to be a banker, but I lost interest" plays on the double meaning of *interest* (the job's financial term and motivation).

## Is a Pun the Same as a Joke?

No, a pun is not the same as a joke, though puns are often used inside jokes as the punchline's mechanism. A joke is a broader structure — usually a setup and a payoff — while a pun is one specific tool a joke can use to deliver that payoff. See [Pun vs. Joke vs. Riddle](/pun-vs-joke-vs-riddle/) for the full comparison.

## Why Do People Make Puns?

People make puns because the moment of recognizing a double meaning triggers a quick, rewarding surprise, which is why puns show up so often in headlines, advertising, and casual conversation. The brief mental double-take is part of what makes the line memorable.
