# Types of Puns: 7 Kinds of Wordplay With Examples

There are 7 main types of puns — homophonic, homographic, homonymic, compound, recursive, visual, and texting puns — each named for how its double meaning is built: by sound, by spelling, by combining both, by structure, or by swapping format entirely.

## What Are Homophonic Puns?

Homophonic puns are puns that rely on two words sounding alike while being spelled differently and meaning different things. "A bicycle can't stand on its own because it's two-tired" plays on *two-tired* sounding like *too tired*. Read the full [homophonic puns](/homophonic-puns/) guide for more examples.

## What Are Homographic Puns?

Homographic puns are puns that rely on one word spelled the same way but carrying two different meanings, sometimes with different pronunciations. "I used to be a baker, but I couldn't make enough dough" plays on *dough* meaning both bread ingredient and money. Read the full [homographic puns](/homographic-puns/) guide for more examples.

## What Are Homonymic Puns?

Homonymic puns are puns that combine both homophonic and homographic mechanisms at once — the word sounds alike, is spelled alike, and still carries two separate meanings. "The past, present, and future walked into a bar — it was tense" plays on *tense* as both a grammar term and an emotional state. Read the full [homonymic puns](/homonymic-puns/) guide for more examples.

## What Are Compound Puns?

Compound puns are puns that stack two or more individual puns into a single sentence, so the line rewards the reader more than once. A compound pun might layer a food pun and a time pun back to back in the same joke. Read the full [compound puns](/compound-puns/) guide for more examples.

## What Are Recursive Puns?

Recursive puns are puns where the second half only makes sense once the first half has already been understood, building the joke in two connected steps rather than one. The humor depends entirely on remembering the setup by the time the punchline lands. Read the full [recursive puns](/recursive-puns/) guide for more examples.

## What Are Visual Puns?

Visual puns are puns that use an image instead of words to create the double meaning, relying on what's pictured rather than what's written. A logo shaped to suggest two different objects at once is a classic visual pun. Read the full [visual puns](/visual-puns/) guide for more examples.

## What Are Texting Puns?

Texting puns are puns that use letters, numbers, or shortened spellings to create a double meaning that only works in written form, like "gr8" for "great" or "CU" for "see you." Read the full [texting puns](/texting-puns/) guide for more examples.

## Which Type of Pun Is Easiest to Write?

Homophonic puns are generally the easiest type to start with, since they only require finding one word that sounds like another, rather than managing spelling, structure, or image-based tricks at the same time.
